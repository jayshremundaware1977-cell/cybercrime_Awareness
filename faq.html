Questions? We've Got Answers.

Find simple explanations about phishing, passwords, online fraud, suspicious links, reporting and everyday cyber safety.

Phishing is a type of social engineering in which attackers try to trick people into revealing information, clicking harmful links, installing software or making payments.
Check the domain carefully, look for unexpected spelling, unusual subdomains, misleading URLs and unexpected requests for passwords or payment. Avoid opening suspicious links simply to test them.
Use long, unique passwords or passphrases and avoid reusing the same password across important services. A reputable password manager can help manage unique credentials.
Multi-factor authentication adds another verification step in addition to a password. It can significantly reduce the risk of account takeover when enabled correctly.

Contact your bank or payment provider promptly through its official channel, preserve transaction information and report the incident through the appropriate official cybercrime reporting channel.
Be cautious about any request that asks you to approve an unexpected payment or enter a UPI PIN without understanding exactly what transaction you are authorizing. Verify the request before approving it.
For incidents in India, use the official National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal at cybercrime.gov.in. Keep relevant incident, transaction and evidence details ready.
Keep relevant screenshots, URLs, emails, messages, transaction/reference numbers, dates, times and other information that accurately documents what happened. Avoid modifying potentially useful evidence.
Preserve relevant messages, screenshots and other evidence when possible. Do not continue interacting with the suspected scammer simply to collect more information.
Public networks can introduce additional risks. Avoid accessing sensitive services on networks you do not trust, use HTTPS-enabled services and keep your device and security settings updated.
Updates can include security fixes for known vulnerabilities. Keeping operating systems, browsers and applications updated reduces exposure to known security issues.
Avoid publicly exposing sensitive information such as passwords, authentication codes, financial details, identity-document information and other data that could be misused.
Teach children not to share personal information with strangers, to question unexpected messages and to tell a trusted adult when something online makes them uncomfortable or suspicious.

Remember

Three Simple Rules

🛑

Stop

Don't rush when a message creates fear, urgency or pressure to pay or reveal information.

🔎

Verify

Check the sender, website and request using a trusted source before taking action.

🚨

Report

If an incident occurs, preserve relevant information and use an appropriate official reporting channel.
